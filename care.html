Care & repair

Made to last. Meant to be looked after.

A stitched leather shoe is not disposable. With a little care, the pair you wear tonight can still be on your feet in ten years — and resoled as many times as the years ask.

Everyday

Small habits, long life.

Leather is skin. It wants to breathe, to be fed now and then, and to rest between wearings. None of this is complicated; it’s just a habit, like anything worth keeping.

Rest
Let a pair rest a day between wearings. Rotating two pairs will roughly double the life of both.
Trees
Cedar shoe trees hold the shape and take up moisture overnight.
Clean
Wipe dust with a soft brush or cloth before it works into the grain.
Feed
A little cream conditioner every few weeks keeps the leather supple and weather-resistant.
Polish
Wax brings the colour back and gives it depth. Buff, and the patina deepens with age.
Dry slowly
Never by a radiator. Let wet shoes dry at room temperature, stuffed with paper.
Repair

A worn sole is not the end.

Because our soles are stitched — welted or McKay, never glued — they can be replaced. When the sole wears through, any good cobbler can stitch on a new one, and the shoe goes on.

That is the whole point of making them this way. It is also why they cost what they cost: you are buying years, not a season.
