Our story

A dancer, looking for a pair of shoes that fit.

Not a size chart. Not “close enough.” Something made for the foot I actually stand on — and for the way I move.

I came to shoes the way most dancers do — out of frustration. I danced, and I could not find the shoe. Everything was either beautiful and wrong, or comfortable and plain, or simply made for someone else’s foot. So, eventually, I stopped waiting for someone to make it and began learning to make it myself.

That was the beginning of Swing Love. Not a business plan — a dancer who wanted a proper pair of shoes, and who was stubborn enough to keep at it until they existed.

It started with swing

It really began the night I fell for a dance born in the African-American communities of Harlem — the Lindy Hop. After nights of swinging out, it was not only my feet that hurt; my whole body did. And the pain always started at my feet.

A good pair of dance shoes is the foundation of happy feet — and of the easy, happy sleep that follows a night of dancing. Finding a shoemaker here who cared about quality and fit, and who was open to doing things differently, was the missing piece. That first pair solved everything: comfortable, and my whole body felt better after a night of swinging.

That is where the name comes from. Swing, and a love of it.

What has changed

A great deal, over the years. For a long stretch I worked with the same shoemaker here — eight years, side by side, learning from someone who knew the craft long before I did. That chapter has now closed, and we have each gone our own way. I am grateful for every pair that came out of it.

Today I work with a new workshop, closer to the kind of making I want to offer: fewer, better, slower. The tools are the same — the last, the awl, the waxed thread, the hands. The intention is the same too, only clearer now.

What has not

After all of it, I am still making the thing I love. I am still more interested in ten pairs made well than a thousand made quickly. Quality over quantity is not a slogan I reached for — it is simply the only way I know how to work, and the reason the shoes last the way they do.

I am also still, first and last, a dancer. That is where Swing Love began, and it is why the shoes are built to move — to carry you across a floor at midnight and still feel right on the street the next morning.

Where it is going

For years, Swing Love made dance shoes. It still does — most of what we make is for dancers, and always will be. But I have come to believe that a shoe made this carefully does not belong to one room only. So we are opening a second door: bespoke. Made to measure, welted, stitched and patinated by hand, for anyone who loves a well-made shoe — whether or not they have ever danced a step.

Whether you found us on a dance floor or on a quiet hunt for something well made, you are welcome here.

— Châu

The promise

“Quality over quantity is not a slogan. It’s the only way I know how to work.”

Every pair is made to order. Nothing sits on a shelf waiting to be discounted. What we make, we stand behind — and we would rather repair a shoe than replace it.

The name

Why “Swing Love”?

If you are not a swing dancer, you may have wondered about the name.

On the evening of December 30th, 2020 — after a few years of making shoes for myself and for swing-dancer friends, just for fun, as a side hustle — a friend said: why not give it a name, just for fun? I thought about it, and out came Swing Love. My love for swing dancing, put into two words.

When the side hustle became my full-time love, I kept the name — even knowing I would make shoes not only for swing dancers but for anyone who loves a well-made shoe. It reminds me why I began this journey, and why I do what I do.

As the shoes reached beyond the dance floor, that side of the house needed its own name. So our made-to-measure line is called Swing Love Atelier — for anyone who loves a well-made shoe, dancer or not. Same house, same hands; one door opened a little wider.

The mascot

A little character named Ái.

Ái rides around Việt Nam with us — on her red scooter, a basket of shoes up front. She is not the brand; I am. (She looks a little like me, on purpose.)

Think of her as the spirit of the workshop: cheerful, a bit cheeky, always on her way somewhere. She appears now and then — in the journal, on a shoe bag, at the corner of a page. A touch of playfulness, nothing more.
