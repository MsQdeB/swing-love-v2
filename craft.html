Craft

Slowly, by hand, the long way round.

There is a faster way to make a shoe. We are not interested in it.

A pair of our shoes passes through ten or more stages, most of them by hand and several of them twice. Nothing happens on a conveyor belt. The result is a shoe that fits the foot it was made for, holds its shape for years, and can be brought back to life rather than thrown away.

The steps, in order

01 · Fit
We begin with the foot. For made-to-measure, that means measurements and often a trace; for made-to-order, a considered fitting against our lasts.
02 · Last
A wooden last is shaped to the foot. The last is the shoe’s true architecture — everything after is built on it.
03 · Pattern & cutting
Leather is cut by hand, the pattern placed to use the strongest part of the hide.
04 · Closing
The upper is stitched together — flat, then around the heel, with a lining that will soften against the foot.
05 · Lasting
The upper is pulled over the last and held, still wet, until it takes the shape it will keep.
06 · Bottoming
The sole is attached — welted or stitched, never simply glued. This is the decision that determines how long a shoe can live.
07 · Heel & sole finish
Edges are trimmed, sanded and sealed; the waist is shaped by hand.
08 · Patina
Colour is layered on by hand — dyes, creams, wax — until the leather has depth rather than flatness.
09 · Rest
The finished shoes rest on their lasts, the leather settling into its final form.
10 · Finishing
Polishing, laces, and — if you wish — your name engraved into the sole.
Constructions

How the sole is attached, matters.

Most shoes in the world are cemented — glued. It is fast, and it is why so many shoes cannot be repaired. We build ours to be resoled. There are two ways we do it.

Goodyear welted
The upper is stitched to a strip of leather — the welt — which is in turn stitched to the sole. The stitches never pass through the insole, so the shoe is water-resistant, sturdy and endlessly resoleable. Slightly heavier, and worth it.
McKay stitched
A chain stitch runs through the upper, insole and sole directly. Slimmer and lighter than a welt, with more flex — easier on a dancer’s foot. Still repairable in the hands of a good cobbler.
Hand patina
The finish is not painted on at the tannery. It is built up here, by hand, in layers — so the colour moves and deepens with the leather, and no two pairs are ever quite the same.

What goes in

Full-grain & top-grain leather
We do not use “genuine leather” — the lowest grade, mixed with scraps and plastic. We source full-grain and top-grain only: the best leather there is for footwear.
Brocade from Nghệ An
Our woven fabric comes from a co-operative of Thái women in Nghệ An province — an ethical, traditional source.
A leather insole & padding
Under your foot: a leather insole, a layer of arch support, and a bed of padding for comfort.
Stacked leather heels & outsoles
Cut and stacked by hand — a decades-old practice that makes the sole durable and, when the day comes, resolable.
Upcycled shoe bags
Each shoe bag is sewn from upcycled fabric by our local women tailors, and carries a leather badge cut from our leftover leather.
Water-coconut wrapping
Our wrapping paper is handmade from the water-coconut plant — and it is disposable.
Why Việt Nam?

Not because it is cheaper. Because this is where the hands are.

“Made in Vietnam” has come to mean cheap and fast. We would like to help change that.

Our shoes are neither cheap nor quick. They are cut and lasted by hand, in small batches, by people paid properly for work worth doing. The leather is full-grain, the soles are stitched, and the whole pair is built to be resoled rather than replaced.

We are proud to make here. Vietnam is not where we go to save money — it is where our craft lives.

Why it lasts

A shoe you can keep.

Because the sole is stitched, it can be replaced. Because the upper is leather, it can be fed and polished back to life. Because it was made for a foot and not an average, it does not need breaking in as much as it needs to be lived in.
